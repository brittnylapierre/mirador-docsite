# Comparing Items in a Multi-Window Workspace

The primary design principle behind Mirador is **comparative research**. Unlike single-image viewers, Mirador provides a flexible multi-window workspace that allows scholars, conservators, and researchers to place multiple digital objects side by side, zoom in on corresponding details, and cross-reference metadata in parallel.

![Multi-Window Workspace Comparison](/img/user-guide/multi-window-side-by-side.png)

---

## Adding Resources to the Workspace

To open multiple objects in your workspace:

1. Click the **Add Resource** button (`＋`) in the upper-left corner of the workspace toolbar.
2. The **Resource Catalog** opens as a dialog overlay:

![Add Resource Catalog](/img/user-guide/add-resource-catalog.png)

3. From this dialog, you can:
   - **Select Preloaded Manifests**: Click any existing manifest or collection loaded in the catalog (e.g., selecting *Volume 2* alongside *Volume 1*).
   - **Add New Manifest by URL**: Paste the URL of any valid IIIF Presentation Manifest or Collection into the text input and click **Add**.
4. Once selected, Mirador automatically tiles the new window beside your existing windows.

## Workspace Layouts: Mosaic vs. Elastic

Mirador offers two distinct workspace layout paradigms depending on your research needs:

![Select Workspace Type Menu](/img/user-guide/workspace-type-menu.png)

To switch between layout modes, click the **Workspace settings** icon (`⚙`) in the top workspace toolbar and choose **Select workspace type**:

### 1. Mosaic Mode (Tiled Grid)

**Mosaic mode** is the default layout. It organizes windows into clean, non-overlapping rows and columns:

- Windows automatically resize to share the screen equally (e.g., 50/50 split for two windows, 2x2 grid for four windows).
- Dividers between windows can be dragged horizontally or vertically to adjust the proportions of each pane.
- Perfect for side-by-side comparative inspection of two book volumes, historical editions, or infrared vs. visible light photography.

### 2. Elastic Mode (Freeform Canvas)

**Elastic mode** provides an infinite 2D canvas where windows can be freely moved, stacked, resized, and arranged:

- Windows can overlap like desktop applications.
- Drag any window by its header to position it anywhere in the workspace.
- Drag the edges and corners of windows to customize dimensions independently.
- Pan and zoom the entire workspace canvas to manage large numbers of open resources simultaneously.

## Managing Individual Windows

Each window in the workspace operates independently, with its own zoom level, pan position, canvas sequence, and sidebar state.

### Maximizing a Window

When you need to focus on a single object in full detail without closing your other windows:

1. Click the **Maximize window** button (`□`) in the window's top-right header.
2. The selected window expands to fill the entire workspace viewport:

![Window Maximized](/img/user-guide/window-maximized.png)

3. To return to your multi-window layout, click the **Restore window** button (`⧉`). All other windows return to their previous positions and zoom states.

### Closing a Window

When you finish inspecting an object:

1. Click the **Close window** button (`✕`) in the upper-right corner of the window.
2. Mirador closes the window and automatically redistributes the remaining workspace area among the remaining windows:

![Window Closed](/img/user-guide/window-closed.png)

## Saving and Sharing Workspace Sessions

Mirador allows you to preserve your multi-window comparative setup:

![Workspace Options Menu](/img/user-guide/workspace-options-menu.png)

1. Click the **Workspace options** button (`⋮`) in the workspace control panel.
2. Choose **Export workspace** to download or copy a JSON configuration string of your exact layout, open manifests, canvas indices, and viewport coordinates.
3. To reload a previously saved session or share it with a colleague, click **Import workspace** and paste the JSON configuration.
