# Using Collections and Table of Contents

Many historical, academic, and archival digital objects are too extensive to fit into a single IIIF manifest. Institutions organize these works into **IIIF Collections**—hierarchical groupings that contain multiple manifests or sub-collections. Examples include multi-volume encyclopedias, periodical runs, archival series, and themed manuscript collections.

Mirador provides first-class support for browsing IIIF Collections and navigating internal Table of Contents (Ranges) within manifests.

---

## Browsing a IIIF Collection

When you open a IIIF Collection in Mirador (such as the National Library of Scotland's [*Manuscripts containing Middle English texts*](https://view.nls.uk/collections/1334/7486/133474867.json)), the initial window displays the collection-level metadata, summary, and an invitation to browse its contents:

![NLS Collection Overview Window](/img/user-guide/nls-collection-overview.png)

### The NLS Middle English Collection Hierarchy

This collection from the National Library of Scotland contains three major Middle English codices:

1. **Mandeville's travels and other texts**: Includes *Liber maundevyle*, *Sir Cleges*, and *De regimine principum* by Thomas Hoccleve ([Manifest URI](https://view.nls.uk/manifest/1334/7515/133475158/manifest.json)).
2. **Romance and religion**: A collection of romances and religious material, mostly in verse, written in the North Midlands by Richard Heeg ([Manifest URI](https://view.nls.uk/manifest/1336/8006/133680060/manifest.json)).
3. **John of Grimestone**: The preaching-book of a Franciscan friar from Norfolk, written in 1372 ([Manifest URI](https://view.nls.uk/manifest/1336/8036/133680362/manifest.json)).

### Opening the Collection in the Resource Catalog

To browse and select items from a loaded collection:

1. Click the **Add Resource** (`＋`) button in the top-left workspace toolbar.
2. The **Resource Catalog** displays all loaded manifests and parent collections:

![Add Resource Catalog with NLS Collection](/img/user-guide/nls-add-resource-catalog.png)

3. Click on the collection entry (*Manuscripts containing Middle English texts*) to open the collection browser dialog.
4. Inside the collection browser:
   - **Filter Items**: Use the search filter box at the top to filter items by typing part of a title.
   - **Descend & Ascend**: Click any nested sub-collection to descend into its members; click the **Back** button to climb one level up.
   - **Inspect Details**: View the collection's title, description, and rights metadata.
5. Click any manuscript (such as *Mandeville's travels*) to open it in an active window.

> [!TIP]
> **The "Show Collection" Button**: After opening an item through a collection dialog, a **Show collection** button appears in the window sidebar, allowing you to quickly return to browsing the collection. Items opened directly by pasting their manifest URL will not show this button because Mirador does not have a parent collection path in its session state.

## Navigating Manifest Structure & Table of Contents (Ranges)

Once an individual manuscript is opened, you can navigate its structural organization (folios, chapters, sections) via the **Index** companion sidebar.

![Sidebar Index Showing Page 8 Selected](/img/user-guide/nls-sidebar-index.png)

### Accessing the Index Panel

1. Click the **Toggle sidebar** button (`☰`) in the top-left corner of the window.
2. Select the **Index** tab (represented by the book/list icon) in the sidebar tab bar.
3. The Index panel displays the sequential list of all 370 folios in the manuscript.
4. Scroll through the list and click any folio (such as **Page 8** or **Page 9**) to jump the viewer directly to that canvas.
5. In manifests that declare structural ranges (`structures` / `Range`), nested chapter and section headings appear in a collapsible tree structure, allowing chapter-level jumping without scrolling through hundreds of pages.

---

## Chronological Navigation & `navDate` Limitation

Some collections and multi-part documents assign date stamps to canvases using the IIIF `navDate` property (e.g. newspaper runs, diary entries, or photo archives).

> [!NOTE]
> **Timeline Navigation ([IIIF Recipe 0230](https://iiif.io/api/cookbook/recipe/0230-navdate/))**:
> While the IIIF specification supports `navDate` for timeline-based navigation and chronological filtering, Mirador currently ignores `navDate` values on canvases. Navigation through multi-page objects is strictly structural (via the Index or TOC) or sequential.

## Understanding the Hierarchy: Collection vs Manifest vs Canvas

To navigate effectively, keep the following relationships in mind:

```
IIIF Collection (NLS "Manuscripts containing Middle English texts")
 │
 ├── Manifest: Mandeville's travels (Adv.MS.19.1.11 - 370 folios)
 │    ├── Canvas 1: Front flyleaf
 │    ├── Canvas 7: Preceding text
 │    ├── Canvas 8: Page 8 (Folio start)
 │    ├── Canvas 9: Page 9 (Facing folio)
 │    └── Canvas 10...370: Remaining folios
 │
 ├── Manifest: Romance and religion (Adv.MS.19.3.1 - 482 folios)
 └── Manifest: John of Grimestone (Adv.MS.18.7.21)
```

- **Collection**: Groups multiple manifests or sub-collections together.
- **Manifest**: Represents one complete physical or conceptual object.
- **Canvas**: The virtual display coordinate space for an individual page, sheet, or view.
- **Range / Table of Contents**: A curated subset or hierarchy of canvases representing logical divisions.

---

## Best Practices for Large Collections

- **Use the Index for Deep Navigation**: In a 370-page manuscript, scrolling page by page can be slow. Use the Index tab to jump straight to specific folios (e.g. Page 8).
- **Combine with Multi-Window Comparison**: Open two related codices from the collection side by side (e.g., *Mandeville's travels* in Window 1 and *Romance and religion* in Window 2) to compare scribal hands and language dialects.
- **Verify Manifest Compliance**: If a collection fails to render or display its hierarchy, consult the [Mirador IIIF Support Matrix](/iiif-support) to verify that the collection uses valid IIIF Presentation API v2 or v3 specifications.
