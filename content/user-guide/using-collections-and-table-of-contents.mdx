# Using Collections and Table of Contents

Many historical, academic, and archival digital objects are too extensive to fit into a single IIIF manifest. Institutions organize these works into **IIIF Collections**—hierarchical groupings that contain multiple manifests or sub-collections. Examples include multi-volume encyclopedias, periodical runs, archival boxes, and exhibition sets.

Mirador provides first-class support for browsing IIIF Collections and navigating internal Table of Contents (Ranges) within manifests.

---

## Browsing a IIIF Collection

When you open a IIIF Collection in Mirador (such as the *Biological Basis of Medicine* from the Wellcome Collection), the initial window displays the collection-level metadata, summary, and an invitation to browse its contents:

![Collection Overview Window](/img/user-guide/collection-overview.png)

### Opening the Collection Browser Dialog

To explore the manifests in a collection:

1. Click the **SHOW COLLECTION** button in the center of the window or the collection banner.
2. Mirador opens the **Collection Browser Dialog**, listing every volume, part, or child collection declared by the publisher:

![Collection Browser Dialog](/img/user-guide/collection-browse-dialog.png)

3. Review the items listed. In this Wellcome Collection example, six individual volumes are available (*Volume 1* through *Volume 6*).
4. Click any volume to load its manifest directly into the active viewing window.
5. If the collection contains sub-collections (e.g., *Series* > *Sub-series* > *Files*), you can drill down through the hierarchy. Breadcrumbs at the top of the dialog allow you to navigate back up to parent collections.

> [!TIP]
> You can also browse collections later by clicking the **Add Resource** (`+`) button in the top-left workspace toolbar, which lists both individual manifests and parent collections.

## Navigating Manifest Structure & Table of Contents

Once an individual manifest is opened, you can navigate its structural organization (chapters, sections, appendices) via the **Index** companion sidebar.

![Sidebar Index and Table of Contents](/img/user-guide/sidebar-index.png)

### Accessing the Index Panel

1. Click the **Toggle sidebar** button (`☰`) in the top-left corner of the window.
2. Select the **Index** tab (represented by the book/list icon) in the sidebar tab bar.
3. The Index panel displays the logical structure of the object:
   - **Canvases list**: A sequential list of all pages in the manifest with labels (e.g., *Title Page*, *p. 1*, *Plate IV*).
   - **Table of Contents (Ranges)**: When the manifest publisher provides a structural range hierarchy, collapsible chapter and section headings appear in a tree structure.
4. Click any entry in the Index to immediately jump the viewer to that specific canvas.

## Understanding the Hierarchy: Collection vs Manifest vs Canvas

To navigate effectively, keep the following relationships in mind:

```
IIIF Collection (e.g., Multi-volume work: "The Biological Basis of Medicine")
 │
 ├── Manifest: Volume 1 (614 pages)
 │    ├── Canvas 1: Front cover
 │    ├── Canvas 2: Half-title page
 │    ├── Canvas 3: Table of contents
 │    └── Canvas 4...614: Content pages
 │
 ├── Manifest: Volume 2
 ├── Manifest: Volume 3
 └── ...
```

- **Collection**: Groups multiple manifests or sub-collections together.
- **Manifest**: Represents one complete physical or conceptual object.
- **Canvas**: The virtual display coordinate space for an individual page, sheet, or view.
- **Range / Table of Contents**: A curated subset or hierarchy of canvases representing logical divisions (chapters, articles, or sections).

---

## Best Practices for Large Collections

- **Use the Index for Deep Navigation**: In a 600-page book or manuscript, scrolling page by page is inefficient. Use the Index tab to jump straight to specific chapters or plates.
- **Combine with Multi-Window Comparison**: When examining multi-volume sets, open Volume 1 in one window and Volume 2 in a second window to compare editions, indexes, or bibliographies side by side.
- **Verify Manifest Compliance**: If a collection fails to render or display its hierarchy, consult the [Mirador IIIF Support Matrix](/iiif-support) to verify that the collection uses valid IIIF Presentation API v2 or v3 specifications.
