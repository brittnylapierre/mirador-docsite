# Working with Layers and Complex Canvases

Certain digital surrogates—particularly in art conservation, paleography, and cartography—require displaying multiple synchronized images over the same canvas. Examples include multi-spectral scans (natural light, infrared reflectography, ultraviolet fluorescence, X-ray radiography) or historical maps layered over modern satellite imagery.

Mirador natively supports IIIF Presentation v2 and v3 **Choice resources** and multi-painting annotations, giving researchers interactive control over layer opacities and stacking order.

---

## What Are Canvas Layers?

In the IIIF data model:

- A single **Canvas** defines the abstract coordinate space.
- Multiple **Painting Annotations** or a single **Choice** resource attach different image services to that same canvas.
- Rather than opening multiple windows to see different light spectra, all captures align to the exact same pixel coordinates.

---

## Using the Layers Panel

When a loaded manifest includes layered or choice image resources:

1. Click the **Toggle sidebar** button (`☰`) in the window header.
2. Select the **Layers** tab in the sidebar.
3. The Layers panel displays each available image layer with its descriptive label (e.g., *Natural Light*, *Infrared*, *X-Ray Radiograph*).

### Controlling Opacity and Visibility

- **Visibility Checkboxes**: Toggle individual layers on or off to isolate a specific capture.
- **Opacity Sliders**: Adjust the opacity slider of any layer from 0% (fully transparent) to 100% (fully opaque). Blending layers at 50% opacity allows you to reveal underdrawings, hidden text, or palimpsest layers beneath the surface pigment.
- **Layer Stacking Order**: Drag layers up or down in the list to change which image renders on top.

---

## Working with Multiple Sequences

Some complex archival objects contain more than one reading order or image sequence (for example, a manuscript photographed before and after physical conservation, or folded foldouts and gatefolds):

- If a manifest declares multiple sequences, Mirador surfaces a sequence selector in the window or sidebar.
- Switch between sequences to examine alternative curatorial arrangements without losing your zoom state or annotation view.

---

## When to Use Different View Modes

- **Single View**: Best for deep layer blending and close technical examination of a single folio or canvas.
- **Book View**: Ideal for bound volumes where you want to see facing pages in natural spreads.
- **Gallery View**: Best for quickly scanning multi-page items to see which folios feature special multi-spectral or foldout captures.
